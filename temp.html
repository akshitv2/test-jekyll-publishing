Types of Coffee – A Practical Guide

1. Espresso


The foundation of almost all modern café drinks.

  • Volume: 25–30 ml (single shot)
  • Preparation: 7–9 g finely ground coffee, 88–94 °C water, ~9 bar pressure, 25–30 seconds extraction
  • Taste profile: Intense, thick crema, concentrated flavor
  • Synonyms: single espresso, shot

2. Americano


  • Preparation:
    • Espresso + hot water (added after)
    • Popularly served iced
  • Typical ratios:
    • 1:2 (strong) → ~60–90 ml
    • 1:3–1:4 (common) → 120–180 ml
  • Closest filter-coffee-like experience from espresso machine

3. Latte / Caffè Latte


  • Composition: espresso + lots of steamed milk + thin foam layer
  • Typical volume: 240–360 ml (often served in glasses)
  • Most customizable (flavors, oat milk, extra shots…)

4. Flat White


  • Origin: Australia / New Zealand (1980s–90s)
  • Composition: double ristretto/doppio + velvety microfoam (~150–180 ml total)
  • Key difference from latte: much less milk, more coffee-forward, silkier texture

5. Cappuccino


Classic Italian morning drink

  • Classic ratio (roughly equal parts):
    • ⅓ espresso (~30 ml)
    • ⅓ steamed milk
    • ⅓ thick milk foam
  • Total volume: 150–180 ml
  • Often dusted with cocoa or cinnamon

6. Macchiato (“stained” espresso)


Two very different drinks share this name:

Name Region Composition Volume
Espresso Macchiato Italy, Europe espresso + small dollop of foam ~30–40 ml
Caffè Macchiato / Latte Macchiato confusion many countries often = large milk + espresso 200–300 ml

→ Ask before ordering in an international café!

7. Cortado


  • Composition: espresso + roughly equal amount steamed milk
  • Volume: 120–150 ml
  • Character: balanced, less milky than flat white
  • Popular in Spain, Portugal, Latin America

8. Mocha / Mocha Latte / Mocaccino


Latte + chocolate

  • Variants:
    • Dark mocha – more cocoa, less sweet
    • White mocha – white chocolate
    • Often topped with whipped cream

9. Doppio / Double Espresso

  • Volume: 50–60 ml
  • Preparation: double shot (14–18 g coffee)
  • Basically a stronger, longer espresso

10. Ristretto

  • Volume: 15–20 ml
  • Preparation: same dose, shorter extraction (~15–20 s)
  • Character: sweeter, less bitter, more syrupy body

Types of Beans

  • Arabica
    • Market Share: ~60–70% of global production.
    • Flavor Profile: High acidity, sweet, and complex with notes of fruit, sugar, and berries.
    • Characteristics:
      • Contains less caffeine than Robusta.
      • Grows at high altitudes and is susceptible to pests and climate fluctuations.
  • Robusta
    • Market Share: ~30–40% of global production.
    • Flavor Profile:
      • Bold, earthy, and bitter with a grainy or rubbery aftertaste.
      • It produces a thick “crema,” making it popular for espresso blends and instant coffee.
    • Characteristics:
      • Contains nearly double the caffeine of Arabica.
      • It is a hardy plant resistant to disease and can grow at lower altitudes.
  • Liberica
    • Market Share: ~2% of global production.
    • Flavor Profile:
      • Unusual and intense; often described as “woody,” smoky, or floral.
      • The beans are physically larger and asymmetrical.
    • Characteristics: Primarily grown in Southeast Asia (particularly the Philippines and Malaysia).
